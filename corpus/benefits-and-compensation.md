# Benefits and Compensation

This document describes Meridian's benefits and how compensation is structured
and reviewed. Specific figures for your role are in your offer letter and the
People Operations portal.

## Compensation Structure

Compensation consists of a base salary, an annual bonus, and equity. Base salary
is benchmarked against the 75th percentile of the market for each role and
location, using two external salary surveys. Salaries are reviewed once per year
during the annual compensation review in April, and adjustments take effect from
the May payroll.

The annual bonus targets 10% of base salary for individual contributors and 20%
for senior leadership. It is tied to company performance and individual impact,
and is paid in March for the previous calendar year.

## Equity

All permanent employees are granted equity on joining, vesting over four years
with a one-year cliff. After the cliff, equity vests monthly. Additional equity
refresh grants may be awarded during the annual review for strong performance.

## Pension and Retirement

Meridian contributes 6% of base salary to your pension when you contribute at
least 3%, and contributes 4% regardless of whether you contribute. Contributions
are made through payroll. In the US, the equivalent is a 401(k) with a 6% match.

## Health and Wellbeing

All employees and their dependants receive private medical insurance at no cost,
covering GP access, specialist referrals, and mental-health support. Meridian
also provides:

- An annual wellbeing allowance of £600 for gym membership, fitness equipment, or
  wellbeing apps.
- Access to a confidential Employee Assistance Programme, available 24/7.
- Two paid volunteering days per year.

## Referral Bonus

Employees who refer a candidate who is hired and completes probation receive a
referral bonus of £2,000, paid after the new hire's probation ends. There is no
limit on the number of referrals.

## Salary Review and Promotion

Promotions may happen at any time, but compensation changes tied to promotion are
normally applied at the annual review. A promotion requires a written case
supported by your manager and a review panel of senior engineers or leaders in
the relevant discipline. Being denied a promotion in one cycle does not prevent
you from being put forward again in the next.
